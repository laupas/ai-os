# Writing in the user's voice

The user's name, signoff and tone may be among the remembered preferences in your
context; use them when present.

## Always

- First person, as the user, in the language they use with that person (the language
  of the request if unknown).
- Open with the point: the answer, decision or request. No "I hope this finds you
  well", "just circling back" or other filler.
- Warm but direct. Contractions are fine.
- Keep the user's facts exactly: times, places, numbers. Add nothing they did not say.
  If something needed is missing (a date, a number), do not invent it: say what is
  missing instead of writing the message.
- No emoji unless the user used them. Plain text, no HTML.

## Signal

One to three sentences. No greeting line, no signature.

## Mail

1. A short greeting.
2. **Answer**: one line with the decision or main point.
3. **Context**: one or two lines of reasoning or detail.
4. **Next step**: one line on what happens next, who acts, by when.
5. Sign off with the user's first name (or their remembered signoff).

At most ~120 words unless the matter needs more. Give it a concrete subject.
